# Names

A replacement for "Paper Tour". Checked on 2026-10-28.

## Why change it

"Paper Tour" is nine letters and three syllables, and it is easy to forget. It also says "paper" and "tour". The
product is used on exercise sheets, slides, leases and letters as well as papers, and a "tour" describes the format, not
the feeling of being taught. A better name is short, easy to say in English and German, and points at the pen, the light
or the act of explaining.

## How this was done

- **Generated 104 candidates** across styles: invented words, short real words, compounds, verbs, and names with an .ai
  or app feel. They are all in this file: the ten checked ones in the table, the other 94 in the list at the end.
- **Filtered to ten** by these tests: one or two syllables, about eight letters or fewer, distinctive, linked to
  pointing, a pen, light or explaining, easy in English and German, no awkward meaning in German.
- **Checked the ten** with web searches for existing education or AI products with the name, and by fetching the .com,
  .ai and .app homepages to see whether each is live, parked or for sale. I cannot run a WHOIS or register anything, so
  domain notes are weak evidence. "Appears taken" means the page is live, parked or for sale. "No obvious use found"
  means the page did not load or resolve, which does not prove the domain is free.

## What the check found

Almost every short English word that fits is already an AI or education product, often a funded one. Two names that
looked strong were ruled out by the check: Docent (an open-source AI tool that turns a research paper into narrated
slides, so a direct overlap) and Trace (a Y Combinator-backed AI startup with a $3M seed round). Gist, Crux, Glint,
Inkling and Klar also have products in AI or education. The three below have no prominent product of the same kind,
but each has a smaller overlap, noted under it. No name here is clean, so check the trademark registers (EUIPO
TMview, DPMA; classes 9, 41 and 42) before committing.

## Top 3

### 1. Nib

- **Tagline:** The pen that reads it with you.
- **Why it beats Paper Tour:** One syllable and three letters against three syllables and nine. It names the one tool
  the product is built around: the pen tip that points, circles and underlines. It does not say "paper", so a lease or
  an exercise sheet is not a misfit. It is easy to say and spell in German and carries no German meaning to trip over.
- **In a sentence:** "Open the lease in Nib." "Ask Nib why the formula divides by the square root of d_k." As a verb it
  is strained: "Nib this paper" can be learned but does not come naturally.
- **Risks:** A small AI notes app called Nib exists, and nib is an Australian health insurer. Many German students will
  not know "nib" as a word, so the pen meaning has to come from the logo and tagline. nib.com and nib.ai are parked for
  sale; nib.app did not resolve.

### 2. Penlight

- **Tagline:** A pen of light that points at what matters.
- **Why it beats Paper Tour:** One familiar word holds both halves of the product, the tutor's pen and the sentence that
  lights up. It sits next to "highlighter", which every student knows, and reads as a better one. German speakers
  understand it without translation (pen plus light). Two syllables against three.
- **In a sentence:** "Penlight this paper." "Run the lease through Penlight." As a past tense ("I penlit my lease") it is
  awkward, so "ran it through Penlight" works better.
- **Risks:** In medicine a penlight is the small torch used to check pupils, and an iPhone app of that name does this.
  A "Penlight AI" is referenced by a PyPI package, and penlight.app is a live, login-only product. penlight.com is
  parked. At eight letters it is the longest of the three.

### 3. Lupe

- **Tagline:** Take any document under the Lupe.
- **Why it beats Paper Tour:** "Lupe" is German for magnifying glass, and "etwas unter die Lupe nehmen" means to look at
  something closely, which is what the product does to a figure or a clause. Four letters, two syllables, and the round
  shape of a magnifier matches the circle that the pen draws. No product called Lupe was found in education or AI.
- **In a sentence:** "Put this lease under the Lupe." "Lupe this paper" is possible but only makes sense if you know the
  German idiom.
- **Risks:** English speakers do not know the word and may say "loop" or "LOO-pay" (German is LOO-peh). It is weaker
  than the other two on pen and teaching. Near names: PDF Loupe (an AI PDF reader), Lupa (AI veterinary software) and
  LUP (a smart magnifier). lupe.app is parked for sale, lupe.ai is registered, lupe.com did not resolve.

If the team values meaning over punch, swap 1 and 2: Penlight explains itself, Nib is shorter.

### Invented words

- Peka: Swedish "peka" means to point; said three ways in Swedish, English and German. Searched: nothing found.
- Pointa: "point" with a soft ending; sounds like a pointer, not a brand.
- Nibo: Nib with a friendly ending; easier to own, less punchy.
- Lumeo: from Latin lumen, light; close to "Romeo".
- Dexo: from Latin dexter and index, the pointing finger; sounds technical and says little.
- Zeko: pure sound, short and snappy, no meaning to carry.
- Glimo: Glim with an -o; light and playful.
- Docta: Latin "doctus", taught or learned; also Spanish "docta".
- Lampo: Italian for a flash; echoes German "Lampe". Searched: nothing exact, a near miss in Lampi AI.
- Sema: Greek for sign or mark; short but vague.
- Skribo: "writing" in Esperanto, same root as scribe; pen feel, odd spelling.

- Illumi: from "illuminate"; a generic tech-brand sound.
- Aimly: aim plus -ly; a typical app suffix with little character.
