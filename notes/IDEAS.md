# Ideas from the 10-29 exploration

Eight read-only agents each studied one direction (code, real tours, the 13 test documents) and ranked proposals by value (1-5) and effort (S, M, L). This is the condensed list; the roadmap that picks from it is in [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md).

## Research papers

A paper is an argument to audit, not a script to replay (slides) or a procedure to demonstrate (exercise sheet). The tutor's job is to line up each claim with its evidence, show why it should work, say how far the evidence reaches, and expose the seams. The harness does not do this today. paperPlan fills card slots (1 problem, 1 idea, up to 4 method steps, 3 figures, 3 results, 2 limitations) and, in deep, sorts them by page.

1. **Plan the tour from claims and their evidence (the four-question spine)** (value 5, effort M). The tour is organised by the paper's claims, and each claim is taught in the same four moves: what changed (against what), why it should work, the evidence and how far it reaches, the catch.
2. **Narration moves: conclusion first, one because, numbers with their comparison, never an echo** (value 5, effort S). Every step has one move, assigned by the plan, and the voice follows that move's grammar. The learner hears the conclusion first, then one 'because', then one number with its comparison, then quiet while the visual plays. Illustrative rewrites from the real run.
3. **Table intelligence: read a table into a typed grid, let code derive the views, let the model choose** (value 5, effort M). The learner sees each table the way the paper means it. 'Better and cheaper' is a cloud of points with the Transformer up-left. The ablation is bars of change from the base row, grouped by what was varied, with the no-change row marked as the surprise.
4. **The catch, found by code and told once: exceptions, authors' own limits, scope strip** (value 5, effort M). One short, honest step per tour (two in deep), 'Where to be careful', that separates who is speaking. 'The authors say' puts a highlight on their own limitation sentence. 'The table shows' puts a box on the exceptional cell ('Dyer 91.7 against 91.3'). 'Not reported in the text we read' appears as a dashed chip.
5. **Equation inventory: expose unnumbered and inline formulas, rank by centrality, pick a spine** (value 4, effort S). Equations that carry the paper's ideas get a board even when unnumbered. The sine/cosine positional encoding becomes a heatmap of positions by dimensions (VISUALS-V2's heatmap with 'expression'), and multi-head becomes a shapes board.
6. **Hand the learner the knob: a slider on plots, labelled with the paper's own hyperparameter** (value 4, effort S). When a plot step ends (or the learner pauses), the sweep becomes a slider labelled with the paper's own symbol. Drag d_k and watch the softmax saturate. Drag the coupling parameter and watch the vacuum energy collapse.
7. **Teach each kind of evidence with its own lens (and its own visual vocabulary)** (value 4, effort M). Each element is taught the way its kind needs, so the tour has different kinds of explanation, not 21 variations of 'the authors describe'. Method figure: trace one input through the measured parts in order (shapes or scene with real values, never a flow of the printed labels).
8. **Bookend boards: an opening 'delta card' and a closing claim ledger** (value 4, effort M). The first board of every paper tour is a delta card: was, now, gain, animated. Old chips are struck through, the new chip slides in, and the gain numbers count up (attention with recurrence and convolution becomes attention alone, giving parallel training and 28.4 BLEU at a fraction of the cost).
9. **Typed, anchored questions with pre-warmed answers (and an optional 'challenge me')** (value 4, effort M). Question chips are typed and each carries a promise. Why this (against which alternative)? What if (remove or vary X)? Does it hold for (scope)? Show me with numbers. What is the weak spot? What is X (a prerequisite)? Tap one and the answer starts within a second, because the best three were answered in the background when the ...
10. **One deep tour, three reading passes, and primers for the reader's level** (value 4, effort M). One tour, three reading passes, chosen at play time. Triage (about 90 s): thesis, headline evidence, catch. Understand (default): adds why and mechanism. Examine: adds ablation, derivation detail, appendix evidence and second examples. Before step 1 a Primer row asks 'new to any of these?' with 3-4 concepts from the paper.
11. **Derivation ladder for equation-heavy and theory papers** (value 4, effort L). For theory papers the learner watches the derivation happen. Each spine equation appears under the previous one (faded), with the part that changed lit and a short move label ('separate variables', 'impose boundary condition', 'regularize', 'take the limit').
12. **A paper scorecard: measure tours with no model calls** (value 3, effort S). A one-command scorecard scores any saved tour against its doc.json and prints a table to compare across harness versions and documents: for example, run it on demo/attention-short/tour.standard-1028.json, the deep run and the next run.

## Exercise sheets

HOW AN EXERCISE TOUR SHOULD DIFFER FROM A PAPER TOUR. A paper tour is a lecture: it follows the document's order, narrates claims and evidence, and the model's words are the product. An exercise tour is a coaching loop per part (read for what is wanted and forbidden, plan, try, hint ladder, check, reveal one step at a time, general method, pitfall, another one): the learner's attempt is the product and the pace is theirs. Parts are chosen by skill, points and dependency, not page position. The truth is checkable, so correctness is shown as ticks made by code, not asserted by a voice.

1. **Coach mode: Your-turn checkpoints, a hint ladder and reveal-on-tap** (value 5, effort M). Each taught part becomes: (1) a short plan step that points at what is given, asked and forbidden without reading it back ('what kind of problem is this, what would you try first?');
2. **Check my working: the learner's own lines verified in code, first wrong line pointed at** (value 5, effort M). A scratchpad under the page: the learner types or dictates working, one line per row, with live KaTeX preview. Each row gets a badge in about 100 ms: green tick (consistent with the solution), red cross with the code's reason in plain words ('does not hold for x = 11: left 26, right 27'), grey question mark ('I cannot check ...
3. **Matrix-native boards: row reduction, Falk matrix product and 2x2 transformation, all computed by code** (value 5, effort M). Three boards, each one kind in the existing registry. gauss: the augmented matrix with the pivot cell ringed and each row operation written as a label ('R2 <- R2 - 2 R1'), every state animating from the previous, ending in RREF or [I | A^-1], with a read-off line ('x3 free: solution = (...) + t(...)').
4. **Probability boards: natural-frequency dots, Bayes table, tree and a sample-space enumerator that checks the answer** (value 5, effort L). Four boards around one idea, making conditioning visible. (1) population: 10,000 taxis as dots, split blue/green, then who the witness calls blue; the dots that say blue light up and the learner sees 99 of 297 (one third), not 99%. (2) bayes: hypotheses as rows, columns prior, likelihood, product, posterior;
5. **Derivation board: lines accumulate, each with a code-made check mark** (value 4, effort M). A solution reads like a clean blackboard: each step adds one line under the previous ones, the tokens that changed flash (the term that moved, the number that changed), a margin note says why ('subtract 2x on both sides'), and a badge at the right shows green tick 'checked', amber 'could not check', or red 'does not hold'.
6. **Verifier v2: cover what real sheets contain and recompute the answer independently** (value 4, effort M). A checker the learner can trust across subjects, in this order. Fix the crash (one line). Treat P(...), E[...], Var, \Pr, \mathbb{P} as named atoms assigned by lines like variables, so Bayes chains verify arithmetically. Add rank, dim ker, trace and linear independence via exact Gauss.
7. **Sheet analysis, a skill roadmap and on-demand tutoring for any task** (value 4, effort M). The sheet tour becomes a roadmap plus a few teaching moments, and everything else is one tap away. A cheap text-only analysis call labels every part: skill family ('Bayes update', 'matrix product', 'linear system over a finite field'), command kind (compute, prove, decide, state, code), difficulty 1-3, minutes, depends_on, ...
8. **Given-fidelity: recover matrices from glyph geometry and cross-check what the model transcribed** (value 4, effort S). The learner sees the page's matrices typeset beside the printed ones ('Is this what the sheet says?') and the solution is built on code-recovered givens. When the PDF has no usable glyphs, the model transcribes and code cross-checks the transcription against the numerals the text layer holds in those blocks;
9. **Pictures that fit the maths: curves with computed roots and extrema, line systems, number lines** (value 4, effort M). Each part may add one picture computed from the same numbers as the checked solution. Calculus: the curve with roots, critical points and local max/min marked, a tangent sliding to the extremum (slope zero), shaded area for integrals. Systems: the lines, intersection marked;
10. **German and multilingual sheets: clean text layer, correct points, spoken maths, bilingual mode** (value 4, effort M). German, French, Spanish and Dutch sheets get the same tutor quality: clean text, correct points, a task kind per part from a multilingual lexicon (which also picks the Your-turn type: value entry, proof skeleton, or choice), spoken maths in the sheet's language ('x hoch zwei', 'Determinante von A', 'Spaltenvektor'), tray and ...
11. **Read the question properly: constraints, command words and a problem-to-symbols ledger** (value 4, effort M). The first step of a part is about reading, not repeating. The tutor highlights givens (blue), the ask (yellow) and the trap (pink: constraints and required form) and says only what the text does not: 'this is a decision with a justification, and a basis is not allowed'.
12. **Pitfalls, the general method and a practice twin with a verified answer** (value 4, effort M). Per skill family (proposal 7) a method card: when you see this (the cue phrase, highlighted on the page), do this (3-4 steps), check it like that (substitute; count dimensions; probabilities sum to one).

## Visual vocabulary

WHAT I SAW. Today the vocabulary is numbers (bars, scatter, heatmap), boxes (flow), symbols (formula, shapes) and curves (plot). On the real Attention tours that yields 15 bar boards in 21 steps (niko2-deep-run steps 2, 5, 10, 11, 13, 15, 18-20: toy bars such as pos1 10 / pos2 0, attention weights 0.622/0.378, the same EN-DE BLEU three times) and 5 flows in 11 steps (tour.standard-1028 steps 3, 4, 5, 7, 8).

1. **Plot v2: curves that answer questions (marks computed by code, parametric and polar, log axes, data points, drag-to-explore)** (value 5, effort M). The learner sees the function and the fact the paper states about it, found by the code: a dot drops where two cost curves cross with dashed guides to both axes and 'n = 10'; a shaded area sweeps left to right while its value counts up; a tangent line rotates along the curve while its slope reads out; an asymptote dashes in.
2. **Distribution: shapes of uncertainty that morph, and experiments that converge** (value 5, effort M). One board for 'how things are spread and how that spread changes'. A bell curve slides and narrows as n grows, with the tail beyond a threshold shaded and its probability counting up; the attention scores [4, 2, 0] sharpen or flatten as the scale goes from 1 to 1/sqrt(d_k) while an entropy readout falls;
3. **Timeline and schedule: dates, windows and dependencies that the code resolves** (value 5, effort M). A time axis where notice periods grow as bars, allowed windows appear as shaded bands with a bracket ('28 to 90 days before'), and each rule shows a tick or cross as the narration reaches it. The learner can drag the start date and watch every deadline move.
4. **Anatomy: take one dense statement apart into who, must, what, when, unless** (value 4.5, effort S). The highlighted sentence is lifted apart on the board: words colour by role as the narration names them (Landlord = who, serve a rent review notice = must, 28 to 90 days before the review date = when), each slot slides into a labelled column with a few words on what it means, and the skeleton remains.
5. **Families, doc-type menus and a shared board kit (planner diversity, playable boards, page-linked parts)** (value 4.5, effort M). Tours stop repeating themselves because the planner reasons in families of 'what must the learner see' (relation, change, uncertainty, time, quantity, structure, derivation, text) and never repeats a family back to back.
6. **Derivation: persistent lines, terms that glide, reasons between lines, verified marks** (value 4.5, effort L). 3Blue1Brown-style algebra: the previous line stays dimmed above, the symbols that persist glide to their new place in the next line, what changed flashes, a three-word reason ('subtract 2.5 times row 1') sits between lines, and a small tick appears when the code verified the line or an 'unchecked' mark when it could not.
7. **Quantity flow: waterfall, Sankey and funnel with conservation checks** (value 4, effort M). Quantities that move: a gross-to-net waterfall steps down with a running total; a Sankey shows where a budget, a rent, a compute budget or a patient cohort goes, band width proportional to value; a funnel shows what survives each filter.
8. **Probability: trees, natural frequencies and outcome grids with exact arithmetic** (value 4, effort M). Conditioning becomes visible. A tree grows stage by stage with probabilities on the edges and a token that multiplies along a path; learning the evidence greys out the leaves that no longer fit and the survivors rescale to 100 percent, which is the moment Bayes clicks.
9. **Space: 2D geometry and linear maps (grid warps, eigenvectors, line systems) computed by code** (value 4, effort L). The signature 3Blue1Brown picture: the plane's grid bends smoothly into the image of a matrix, the unit square becomes a parallelogram with its area (the determinant) counting up, eigenvector lines stay put while everything else turns.
10. **Intervals: number lines, forest plots, reference ranges and magnitude rulers** (value 3.5, effort S). A horizontal axis (linear or log) where each row is a point with a whisker, a band marks the normal or allowed zone, and reference lines (zero, a threshold, a limit) cut across.
11. **Compare: diff of two passages (found by code) and a side-by-side matrix with computed winners** (value 3.5, effort M). Two views of one idea: 'what is different'. Diff: the old clause fades, struck words slide out, inserted words grow in, and chips list every number that changed ('28 to 14 days').
12. **Decision path: 'does this apply to me?' as a branching board the learner can walk** (value 3.5, effort M). A branching board: the document's own rule drawn as questions with labelled answers ending in outcomes, with an example case walked by a moving token while the narration says it. When paused, the learner clicks their own answers and the board lights their route to an outcome;

## Learning and interaction

Today the product is one very good monologue. The document type picks an arc and a tone (doctype.ts TONE, arcs.ts ARC_SPECS) and the learner presses Create. After that they only listen. Their one lever is a typed or spoken question that costs a full model call (STATUS.md: 17.8 s and 31.966 s; ask.ts allows 180 s and two attempts), keeps its history in React state (AskPanel `history`, MAX_HISTORY 4, answers clipped to 700 characters in the prompt) and ends with the same three generic chips that stage A wrote before the tour existed (card.ts suggested_questions, arcs.ts:376).

1. **Start with what you need: goal, level and minutes compile into a per-document-type tour** (value 5, effort M). When the learner presses Create tour, a small sheet opens with the answers pre-selected, so one click still works. 'Looks like a lease. Right?' (editable). Goal cards that depend on the type. Paper: Gist, Understand the method, Review it critically (claims against evidence), Build on it.
2. **'Not clear?': instant Simpler, Example, A term and Deeper on every step, from a pre-generated study companion** (value 5, effort M). While a step plays, or when it is paused, a quiet 'Not clear?' button expands into four chips. Simpler: a 30-45 word restatement of the point in plainer words, spoken at once over the same page position, with no new camera move. Example: a tiny worked case with its own board frames, in the existing checked-example format.
3. **Your turn: attempt first, a three-rung hint ladder and answer checking** (value 5, effort L). Goal 'Coach me': the tutor reads the part, boxes the givens on the sheet, and stops: 'Your turn. What would you try first?' The ask dock turns into an answer card with a math-aware text field (live KaTeX preview), a microphone, and Hint, Check and Show me buttons.
4. **Touchable boards: predict, then drag the parameter** (value 4, effort M). The 3Blue1Brown 'pause and ponder' beat. During the narration the tutor drags the slider itself ('now double d_k'); when the narration ends the learner can drag too: the plot probe, the parameter, the inputs of a worked example.
5. **Quick checks that use the page: tap the clause, predict, test out, explain back** (value 4, effort M). At the end of a chapter the dock shows a quiet 'Quick check · 20 s [Try] [Skip]'; never blocking, with a global 'no checks' switch. Kinds, all authored from the chapter's own evidence and graded in code: Tap it ('Where does it say how much notice you must give?': the learner taps the page;
6. **Keep: the takeaway that survives the session (paper card, exam sheet, obligations and dates, to-do) plus saved answers** (value 4, effort M). A 'Keep' card at the end of the tour, and a Notes tab in the sidebar, different for each document type. Paper: a one-page card (claim, problem, idea, three key numbers, limits, 'my open questions') with figure thumbnails, each line linked back to its sentence.
7. **Situations and your own dates and amounts for contracts, letters and forms** (value 4, effort M). Instead of generic suggestions, 4-6 situation chips taken from this document ('I want to leave early', 'I pay late', 'My rent is reviewed', 'I did not get the letter in time'). Each answers neutrally in two or three steps: what the document says happens, the clause boxed on the page, the cost as bars.
8. **Tap anything: instant glossary, notation and 'where is this' without a model call** (value 4, effort M). Tap or click a sentence on the page and a small popover lists the defined terms and symbols in it. Tapping one gives, at once, its plain meaning (at most 20 words) and the camera and pen jump to the sentence where the document defines it. 'Explain more' hands the sentence to the ask pipeline as a selected region.
9. **Answers by question type: fast tiers, verb chips and boards that continue** (value 4, effort M). The learner picks, or the code infers, a verb: Where is this, Define, Explain, Show an example, What if. Where is this answers in under a second: camera, highlight and a one-line template, no model. Define and simple yes-or-no questions get a quick answer: one short call, one cue, no board.
10. **A tutor that remembers: learner state, follow-ups that build, and a map of what was not covered** (value 4, effort M). The tutor behaves as if it was in the room. A follow-up resolves 'that' and 'and for the second one?' by carrying over the previous answer's evidence. Answers say 'you heard this in step 9' in half a sentence instead of repeating it.
11. **A voice tutor: hold V to talk anywhere, spoken commands that act at once, spoken answers to checks** (value 4, effort M). Hold V anywhere in the reader to talk. The tour pauses at once; on release the words are transcribed and, if they were a command, simply obeyed with no model call: 'go back', 'again, slower', 'simpler', 'show an example', 'what does X mean' (instant, from the glossary), 'skip this chapter'.
