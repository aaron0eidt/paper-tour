\section{Purpose}
Artificial intelligence (AI) tools such as chat assistants, code assistants and image generators can make our work
faster and better. They can also leak confidential information, produce wrong results and create legal risk.
This policy sets out how Calder \& Finch Consulting Ltd.\ (``the Company'') uses AI tools safely and responsibly.

\section{Scope}
This policy applies to all employees, interns and contractors of the Company worldwide who use an AI tool for
Company work, on Company devices or on private devices. It covers generative AI tools that produce text, code, images
or audio from a prompt, whether they are provided by the Company or by third parties. It does not cover
the internal analytics models that the Data Science team runs under the separate model governance standard.

\section{Definitions}
\textbf{Approved AI tool:} a tool that appears on the Approved AI Tools List on the intranet and for which the Company
has a contract that excludes training on our data. \textbf{Public AI tool:} any other AI tool, in particular free web
chat services used with a personal account. \textbf{Prompt:} any text, file or image that you enter into an AI tool.

\section{Rules for the use of AI tools}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{4.\arabic*}, leftmargin=2.6em, itemsep=0.4em]
  \item \textbf{Use approved tools.} Use only AI tools on the Approved AI Tools List for Company work. Do not use public AI
        tools for Company work, and do not sign in to AI tools with a private account for work purposes.
  \item \textbf{Respect the data classes.} Check the data class of everything you enter into an AI tool and follow the
        table in section 5. Never enter Restricted data into any AI tool.
  \item \textbf{No client data without permission.} Do not enter client names, client documents or client code into an AI
        tool unless the client contract allows it and the engagement manager has confirmed this in writing.
  \item \textbf{You remain responsible.} AI output may be wrong, biased or out of date. Check facts, figures, code and
        references before you use them. You are responsible for your work, whether or not an AI tool helped.
  \item \textbf{Be transparent.} Tell the client when the contract requires it, and mark AI-generated content in client
        deliverables as the engagement manager directs.
  \item \textbf{No automated decisions about people.} Do not use AI tools to make or prepare decisions about hiring,
        promotion, pay or discipline of individuals without the approval of Human Resources and the Data Protection Officer.
  \item \textbf{Protect intellectual property.} Do not enter source code or documents that are marked ``Confidential'' into
        AI tools other than the Company's enterprise tenant, and do not use AI output that may infringe third-party rights.
  \item \textbf{Report incidents.} Report any suspected leak of data into an AI tool, or any harmful AI output, to
        security@calder-finch.example within 24 hours.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Responsibilities}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em, itemsep=0.25em]
  \item \textbf{Employees and contractors} follow this policy, complete the mandatory training and report incidents.
  \item \textbf{Managers} make sure that their teams know and follow the policy and that new team members complete
        the training within 30 days of starting.
  \item \textbf{IT Security} maintains the Approved AI Tools List, reviews requests for new tools within 10 working days
        and monitors compliance.
  \item \textbf{The Data Protection Officer} carries out a privacy impact assessment before any tool that processes
        personal data is added to the list.
  \item \textbf{Human Resources} runs the disciplinary procedure and includes the training in the onboarding programme.
\end{itemize}

\section{Training}
All employees and contractors must complete the e-learning course ``Responsible AI at Work'' (about 45 minutes) in the
Learning Portal \textbf{by 31 January 2027}. New employees must complete it within 30 days of their start date, and
everyone repeats a short refresher once a year. Access to the approved AI tools is suspended on 1 February 2027 for anyone
who has not completed the course and is restored on the day it is completed.

\section{Consequences of violations}
A breach of this policy can lead to disciplinary action under the Company's Disciplinary Procedure, from a
written warning up to dismissal in serious cases. For contractors, a breach can lead to termination of the contract.
Where the law is broken, for example by unlawful disclosure of personal data, the Company may also report the matter
to the authorities. Employees who report their own mistake within 24 hours will not be disciplined for the report
itself, and the prompt reporting is taken into account.

\section{Exceptions}
Requests for exceptions are sent to the IT Service Desk. Only the Chief Information Security Officer can approve an
exception, in writing and for a maximum of six months.
